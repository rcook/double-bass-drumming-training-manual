Every session opens with warm-up on the current R&L Ch. 1 exercises before you work this chapter. Baseline 30-min session: ~7–8 min warm-up (Ch. 1) → ~15 min focus (this chapter) → ~5–6 min application → ~2 min cool-down. Full detail in `approach.md` §6.

## How to use this sheet

Seventh chapter of Stage 6 (`approach.md` §5). R&L defines the concept plainly: a *turnaround* is a double-bass pattern that switches your downbeat foot to an upbeat and your upbeat foot to a downbeat — the whole feel flips. This chapter has 10 Feet Only Warm-Ups that isolate the switch and 16 Beats that put it into a groove.

R&L's own coaching for this chapter: turnarounds are powerful, so start slowly. Practise on and off the pedals — tapping the pattern on the floor away from the kit counts. Balance matters. Those three cues together are the whole coaching set for the chapter.

The bpm defaults come from the feet-only row of `approach.md` §4 (start 60 bpm, target ~120 bpm) for the Warm-Ups, and the 16th-note beats row (start 50 bpm, target ~110 bpm) for the Beats.

## Practice notes

- Start slowly. R&L says so directly and the concept — flipping downbeat and upbeat feet — is exactly the kind of pattern that goes bad fast if you rush it.
- Off-kit reps count. R&L is explicit about this in this chapter as well.
- Balance under a turnaround is not automatic. If the switch is throwing you forward or back, the pattern is not clean at this bpm regardless of what the metronome says.
- Do not open this chapter until Stage 2 core beats hold ~90 bpm — the turnaround is a feel-level modification on top of the underlying groove, and the underlying groove has to work first.
