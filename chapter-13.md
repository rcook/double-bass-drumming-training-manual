# R&L Ch. 13—Linear Cross-Rhythm Combinations

- **Stage:** 5 (Applications)
- **Book pages:** R&L p. 69
- **Total exercises:** ~25 grouping combinations, split between 16th-note groupings (single bar) and triplet groupings (two bars of 8th-note triplets, or one bar of 16th-note triplets)
- **Section labels present:** none—the page is organised as a table of grouping combinations

**Session role:** focus block.

Every session opens with warm-up on the current R&L Ch. 1 exercises before you work this chapter. Baseline 30-min session: ~7–8 min warm-up (Ch. 1) → ~15 min focus (this chapter) → ~5–6 min application → ~2 min cool-down. Full detail in [approach.md §6](approach.md#6-the-session-template).

## How to use this sheet

This is a one-page follow-on to Ch. 12 and is generally worked alongside Ch. 12 rather than as a standalone chapter. It is not a standalone drill chapter. R&L presents a set of number-string combinations that add up to 16 (for one bar of 16th notes) or 24 (for two bars of 8th-note triplets, or one bar of 16th-note triplets). Each number is the size of a linear cross-rhythm grouping. R&L notes that the possibilities are limitless—the listed combinations are seeds, not a checklist.

The chapter is best worked as an extension to Ch. 12: once a specific grouping (say the 5-note grouping) feels stable in Ch. 12, come here and pick a combination that puts that grouping next to another one (say 5-5-3-3, or 3-5-5-3), then play the resulting fill as a one-bar phrase. R&L points out that the order of numbers greatly affects the sound; two combinations with the same numbers in different orders are different exercises.

The bpm defaults come from the 16th-note beats row of [approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule) (start 50 bpm, target ~110 bpm) for the 16th-note groupings, and the triplet-material row (start 40 bpm, target ~110 bpm) for the triplet groupings.

## Suggested starting combinations

Pick one and hold it for the 1-minute clean pass. Rotate combinations across sessions.

- **3-3-3-3-4 (16th notes).** Four consecutive 3-note groupings plus a closing 4-note grouping—a clean introduction to combining lengths.
- **5-5-3-3 (16th notes).** Two 5-note groupings then two 3-note groupings—the same total, more shape.
- **7-7-2 (16th notes).** Two 7-note groupings and a 2-note tail—heavily displaced against the pulse.
- **9-7 (16th notes).** One 9-note grouping and one 7-note grouping—a full bar in two groupings.
- **4-4-4-4-4-4 (triplet).** Six 4-note groupings across two bars of 8th-note triplets—an even reference case before the odd combinations.
- **5-5-5-5-4 (triplet).** Four 5-note groupings and a 4-note tail—classic five-against-triplet displacement.
- **7-7-7-3 (triplet).** Three 7-note groupings and a 3-note tail—dense.

## Practice notes

- Do not open this page before Ch. 12's grouping warm-ups feel stable. This chapter presumes you already have the 3, 5, 7 and 9 groupings in the hands and feet.
- R&L is explicit that the order of numbers matters. If a combination feels off, try the same numbers in a different order.
- Any of the previously covered cross-rhythm phrases can be plugged into these combinations. Think of this chapter as an index of fill *shapes* rather than a source of new rhythms.
- Two-measure triplet phrases (the 24-triplet combinations) are the right target for the 1-minute clean pass at slow bpm—a single bar is too short to hear the cross-rhythm resolve.

## Log line format

Log this chapter's work with the combination in place of an exercise number—for example `13.5-5-3-3  50 bpm  pass  clean` or `13.7-7-7-3-tri  40 bpm  near  drifting`. See [approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking).
