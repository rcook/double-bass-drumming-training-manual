## How to use this sheet

This is a survey chapter. Stage 5 ([approach.md §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression)) puts it here as an application. The goal is exposure to the linear-cross concept—a state where no two limbs strike at the same time, arranged into odd groupings that do not line up with the underlying pulse—rather than mastery of every grouping.

R&L introduces the linear concept plainly: none of your limbs hit at the same time. Cross-rhythms are usually odd note groupings (3, 5, 6, 7, 9) played repetitively so they do not coincide with the downbeats of even-note groupings; even-note cross-rhythms are possible too (see Triplet 4s further into the chapter). All the fills here use linear cross-rhythms. R&L encourages orchestrating the hands between snare and toms; playing one hand on a tom sounds particularly good.

Do not try to work every exercise. Pick one sub-section per session, sit with a couple of exercises inside it, and move on. Rotate sub-sections across sessions.

The bpm defaults come from the 16th-note beats row of [approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule) (start 50 bpm, target ~110 bpm) for the 16th-based groupings, and the triplet-material row (start 40 bpm, target ~110 bpm) for the triplet-based sub-sections. Because the placement is odd-against-even, the real starting bpm will often be lower.

## The sub-sections of this chapter

Work them in book order, but do not treat the list as a checklist. One sub-section at a time.

- **3/16 Grouping Warm-Ups**—16 short warm-ups that establish the 3-against-4 feel. R&L says: practise the grouping warm-ups first, before applying them in one- or two-bar phrases. Suggested subset: 1 and 5 as core; the rest as rotation.
- **Three-Note Rhythms**—16 fills built on the 3-note cross-rhythm. R&L notes that a three-note linear cross-rhythm can be three 16ths *or* any rhythm that fits into the space of three notes. Suggested subset: 1 and 5.
- **5/16 Grouping Warm-Ups**—15 warm-ups on the 5-against-4 feel. Suggested subset: 1 and 7.
- **Five-Note Linear Cross Rhythms**—14 fills on the 5-note grouping. Suggested subset: 1 and 5.
- **6/16 Grouping Warm-Ups**—14 warm-ups on 6-note groupings. Suggested subset: 1 and 9.
- **Six-Note Linear Cross Rhythms**—14 fills on the 6-note grouping. Suggested subset: 1 and 5.
- **7/16 Grouping Warm-Ups**—14 warm-ups on 7-note groupings. Suggested subset: 1 and 5.
- **Seven-Note Linear Cross Rhythms**—14 fills on the 7-note grouping. Suggested subset: 1 and 3.
- **9/16 Grouping Warm-Ups**—15 warm-ups on 9-note groupings. Suggested subset: 1 and 5.
- **Nine-Note Linear Cross-Rhythms**—15 fills on the 9-note grouping. Suggested subset: 1 and 5.
- **Four-Note-Triplet Linear Cross-Rhythms**—12 fills on 4-note groupings inside a triplet feel. Suggested subset: 1 and 5.
- **Five-Note-Triplet Linear Cross Rhythms**—9 fills on 5-note-in-triplet groupings. Suggested subset: 1 and 5.
- **Seven-Note-Triplet Linear Cross-Rhythms**—8 fills on 7-note-in-triplet groupings. Suggested subset: 1 and 3.

## Practice notes

- The chapter is a survey. If a sub-section feels stuck, move to a different odd grouping rather than grinding—the point is exposure to the pattern of odd-against-even, not mastery of one exercise.
- R&L is explicit that some linear cross-rhythms include a printed sticking as a suggestion; you are free to come up with your own. Try the printed sticking first, then adapt.
- The bass drum rhythms can all be played in unison with the hands on cymbals. R&L notes that the result is no longer strictly linear but is still a cross-rhythm and sounds good—a legitimate variant to try.
- Practise the grouping warm-ups before the fills in a given sub-section. R&L calls this out explicitly for the 3/16 warm-ups but it applies across the chapter.
