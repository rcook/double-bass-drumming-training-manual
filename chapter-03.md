# R&L Ch. 3—Three Consecutive 16th Notes

- **Stage:** 2 (16th-note density ladder, second step)
- **Book pages:** R&L pp. 12–15
- **Total exercises:** 32—Warm-Ups Ex. 3.1–3.4, Beats Ex. 3.5–3.18, Fills Ex. 3.19–3.32
- **Section labels present:** Warm-Ups, Beats, Fills

**Session role:** focus and application blocks. Every session opens with warm-up on the current R&L Ch. 1 exercises before you work this chapter. Baseline 30-min session: ~7–8 min warm-up (Ch. 1) → ~15 min focus (one Ch. 3 exercise) → ~5–6 min application (one Ch. 3 beat or fill) → ~2 min cool-down. Full detail in [Approach §6](approach.md#6-the-session-template).

## How to use this sheet

Second step of the 16th-note density ladder (Stage 2 in [Approach §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression)). Where [Chapter 2](chapter-02.md) taught two consecutive 16ths between the feet, this chapter drills three-note groupings—which is where footing choice starts to matter. R&L notes that the most common way to play three consecutive 16ths is **RLR** (strong-foot lead) or **LRL** (weak-foot lead), and the Ch. 3 Warm-Ups deliberately walk through alternative footings (RLL, LRR, RLR-LRL alternating, RRR-LLL alternating) so you become aware of the possibilities rather than defaulting to one.

Work the Warm-Ups first. They drill the three-note foot grouping in isolation—feet only, no hands—so you can hear evenness across the three 16ths without a groove to hide behind. Only once a Warm-Up variant feels even should you move to the Beats, which put three-16th runs inside a rock groove. Fills are reference material; dip in when your ear points at one or you want variety.

**Each Warm-Up has 6 footing variants in the source.** R&L prints variants 1–6 underneath each Warm-Up: RLR, LRL, RLL, LRR, RLR-LRL alternating, RRR-LLL alternating. Do not attempt every variant on every session—pick one variant, hold it for the clean pass, and rotate variants across sessions. The default is variant 1 on each Warm-Up: RLR for the front-three (Ex. 3.1), LRL for the back-three (Ex. 3.3).

The bpm defaults below come from the 16th-note beats row of [Approach §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule): start 50 bpm, target 110 bpm. [Chapter 2](chapter-02.md)'s Warm-Ups consolidated above their 110 bpm default and had their targets revised upward in that sheet (146 and 130). Ch. 3's Warm-Up targets stay at the 110 default until real session data says otherwise.

**Increment differs by exercise type.** Per [Approach §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule), the Warm-Ups (Ex. 3.1 and Ex. 3.3) are feet-only and advance at **+4 bpm** per two-pass advancement; the Beats and Fills advance at **+2 bpm**. The +2 ladder from 50 lands on the 82 bpm Ch. 4 unlock exactly—no rounding needed. See "Moving on to Ch. 4" below.

## Your first Ch. 3 session

Your first Ch. 3 session is your first session after Ex. 2.10 clears its 82 bpm unlock (two consecutive `pass` sessions at 82 bpm—see [Chapter 2](chapter-02.md#moving-on-to-ch-3)). Session shape stays the three-block template from [Approach §6](approach.md#6-the-session-template) that Ch. 2 established.

1. Open your tracking document ([Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking)). Add R&L Ch. 3 to your active chapters alongside Ch. 1 (and Ch. 2 if you are keeping it active per "Moving on to Ch. 3" in [Chapter 2](chapter-02.md#moving-on-to-ch-3)). Add a per-chapter working-state subsection for Ch. 3 ([Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking))—empty for now; populate rows as you touch each exercise.
2. Sit at the kit with a metronome and a timer within reach. Decide the session duration and mentally scale the block minutes per [Approach §6](approach.md#6-the-session-template).
3. **Warm-up block (~7–8 min in a 30-min session).** Run the standard Ch. 1 Core warm-up per [Approach §7 warm-up block](approach.md#warm-up-block)—Ex. 1.1, Ex. 1.10 and Ex. 1.11 at your current bpms. Log each one.
4. **Focus block (~15 min).** Pick **Ex. 3.1**—the first Ch. 3 Warm-Up (three consecutive 16ths on beat + e + & of every beat, rest on a; RLR footing). Set the metronome to 50 bpm and a 15-minute timer. Apply the hold-and-probe loop from [Approach §7](approach.md#focus-block): hold each rung for a 1-minute clean pass, raise **+4 bpm** if clean. The verdict criterion is evenness across the three 16ths, not speed. Expect to climb several rungs on session 1—50 bpm sits well below the coordination floor you built across Chs. 1 and 2. Add one log entry with the session ceiling: `3.1.1  <bpm> bpm  15 min  <verdict>  <note>` (variant 1 is RLR; the Warm-Up variant goes in the suffix per [Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking)). Update the working state per [Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking); on this first-ever session that means a new row keyed `3.1.1` with `last pass bpm` ← ceiling and `current bpm` ← ceiling directly.
5. **Application block (~5–6 min).** Pick **Ex. 3.5**—the first Ch. 3 Beat (rock beat, one three-16th run per bar on beat 3 + 3e + 3&, plus a single strong-foot kick on beat 1). Set the metronome to 50 bpm and a 5-minute timer. Same hold-and-probe loop at the **+2 bpm** Beat increment. Add one log entry: `3.5  <bpm> bpm  5 min  <verdict>  <note>`. Update the working state.
6. **Cool-down (~2 min).** No metronome, no timer. Play something musical. Nothing to log.
7. Stop.

That is a complete first Ch. 3 session. You have not touched Ex. 3.3, Ex. 3.7 or Ex. 3.12—those come in later sessions as you rotate through the Core. The second-session choices (repeat the same pair, or rotate to a new pair) and the consistency-beats-coverage principle from the first fortnight apply here unchanged—see [Chapter 2](chapter-02.md#your-first-ch-2-session) step 7 and the paragraph that follows.

## Rotating through the Core

The Ch. 3 Core has five exercises but each session's focus block holds one (two on longer days) and each session's application block holds one. A workable pattern for the first two or three weeks, at roughly five sessions a week:

- **Focus** alternates between Ex. 3.1 and Ex. 3.3 (the two canonical Warm-Ups—front-three with RLR, back-three with LRL). One per session.
- **Application** cycles through Ex. 3.5, Ex. 3.7 and Ex. 3.12 (the three Beats, in rising density). One per session.

That is one session per Warm-Up every second day and one session per Beat every third day—enough to accumulate two-pass advancements on each without any single exercise sitting stale for a week.

**Footing-variant rotation on the Warm-Ups.** The Warm-Ups' 6 footing variants are the whole point of the section, and session two is where the rotation starts. When Ex. 3.1 comes up for the second time, swap the variant—session A uses RLR (variant 1), session B uses RLL (variant 3), session C goes to LRR (variant 4), and so on. The clean-pass rule applies per `(exercise, variant)` pair, so the two-pass streak at the current bpm is only valid for the variant you worked both sessions on. Keep variant rotation separate from bpm advancement: do not raise the bpm when you change variants—let each new variant find its own ceiling. In practice this means the per-chapter working-state table carries one row per `(exercise, variant)` pair (see [Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking)): `3.1.1` for RLR, `3.1.2` for LRL, and so on, each with its own current / target / last-pass bpm. Rows accrete as you first touch each variant—the row for `3.1.4` first appears the session you drill variant 4. Picking a variant back up after a few sessions on others is where a one-off `near` is most likely.

The worked advancement examples in [Chapter 2](chapter-02.md#rotating-through-the-core) (one-probe-per-session, multi-probe, mixed verdicts, `last pass > current`) apply unchanged here—the loop mechanics do not depend on which chapter the Core exercise lives in. The one addition is the variant axis above.

## Core (most sessions)

Your focus and application pool for this chapter. Pick one Warm-Up for the focus block and one Beat for the application block each session, rotating across sessions so all five get worked over time. For the chosen exercise, look up your current bpm in the tracking document (or use the starting bpm for the first time), set the metronome, set a timer for the block minutes, play unbroken until the timer stops, log a verdict per the clean-pass rule ([Approach §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule)), then move on to the next block.

**Why these five?** R&L Ch. 3 has 32 exercises but most are variations on the three-note-grouping idea. The two Core Warm-Ups isolate the two placements R&L names as "most common": Ex. 3.1 drills the front-three (beat + e + &) with RLR footing, Ex. 3.3 drills the back-three (e + & + a) with LRL footing. Each exercise carries 6 footing variants in the source; the Core table shows variant 1 as the default. The three Core Beats carry a density ladder from one three-16th run per bar (Ex. 3.5) through two (Ex. 3.7) to four (Ex. 3.12). Ex. 3.12 sits at the top of that ladder—four runs per bar, each straddling the beat boundary—which is why it gates Ch. 4 (see "Moving on to Ch. 4").

## Rotation (some sessions)

Once the Core is stable (say, two weeks of consistent `pass` verdicts across Ex. 3.1, 3.3, 3.5, 3.7 and 3.12), start dropping in a Rotation exercise. Swap it in *for* a Core exercise on a given session (keeping the total block count the same), or add it as a fourth block if you have session time to spare. Do not stack more than one Rotation exercise on the same day; the Core is the main path.

**Cadence.** Aim for each Rotation exercise to get worked once every couple of weeks or so when time allows—about one Rotation slot per practice week at the ~5-days-per-week baseline. That is a target, not a rule: skip Rotation entirely on short days, and a missed fortnight is not a debt. The advancement rule still applies—two consecutive `pass` sessions at the same bpm earn the raise—but the two sessions accrue across weeks rather than back-to-back days.

## Reference (discretionary)

Beats Ex. 3.6, 3.8, 3.10, 3.11, 3.13 and 3.14 are additional one-bar grooves that place a single three-16th run at various positions in the bar; dip in when your ear points at one. Beats Ex. 3.15–3.18 are two-bar phrases that combine three-16th runs with snare syncopation; treat them as phrasing examples, not drills, and hold them until the one-bar Beats hold ~90 bpm. Fills Ex. 3.20 and 3.22–3.28 are additional one-measure fill ideas with three-16th runs in various placements. Fills Ex. 3.29–3.32 are four-bar phrases R&L includes to show different fill lengths; treat them as phrasing examples, not drills. Dip into these when you want variety or when your ear picks one out—do not treat them as a checklist. If you never play them, that is fine.

## Practice notes

- The footing labels under each Warm-Up (RLR, LRL, RLL, LRR, RLR-LRL alternating, RRR-LLL alternating) are the whole point of the section. Do not smear them into one footing you find comfortable.
- Rotate the footing variant across sessions rather than trying every variant in one session. The clean-pass rule applies per `(exercise, variant)` pair.
- The weak-foot-lead variants (LRL on Ex. 3.1; LRR on Ex. 3.3; and the other L-starting entries in each exercise's variant list) will set lower ceilings than the strong-foot-lead variants. That is expected and is the point of drilling them—the ladder is set by the weaker side.
- Three-note groupings often sit around the "and" of a beat. If placement is drifting, drop the bpm and count the subdivision aloud.
- Do not raise bpm until both the strong-lead and weak-lead variants of the same exercise hold a clean pass. If one footing passes and the other `near`s, the exercise has not advanced.
- Try the Fills with different orchestrations. R&L suggests strong hand on snare and weak hand on high tom, or strong hand on floor tom and weak hand on high tom.
- These exercises can be played on the kit or on a practice pad—tapping the pattern on the floor is legitimate practice. See [Approach §7 "Off-kit session"](approach.md#off-kit-session) for how off-kit reps sit against the clean-pass rule.
- **Ex. 3.32 bar 1 edition discrepancy.** The Revised Edition prints an extra hi-hat × at bar 1 beat 2a (three 16th-note hi-hats on 2& + 2a + 3). Bar 2 of the same exercise prints a clean 8th-note hi-hat pattern over the identical foot pattern, and the Original Edition prints both bars as straight 8ths. The manual follows the Original. See [Edition notes](edition-notes.md#ex-332-bar-1-revised-edition).
- If the target bpm feels far away, that is expected. The number is a ceiling, not a schedule.

## Moving on to Ch. 4

Ch. 4 (four consecutive 16ths between the feet—see [Approach §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression)) unlocks when **Ex. 3.12 reaches its unlock bpm of 82 bpm**—two consecutive `pass` sessions at 82 bpm on Ex. 3.12, per the advancement rule in [Approach §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule). Ex. 3.12 is a Beat and advances at +2 bpm; the +2 ladder from 50 lands on 50, 52, 54, …, 80, 82—so 82 is on the ladder and no rounding is needed.

Only one gate exercise, not three. Within-stage transitions inside the 16th-note density ladder (Ch. 2 → 3 → 4 → 5) are quantitative—denser runs of the same subdivision—rather than qualitative, so one checkpoint is enough per [Approach §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression) (unlock scheme, rule 2). Ex. 3.12 is the gate because it has the highest three-16th-run density per bar of any Ch. 3 Core exercise—four runs per bar, each straddling the beat boundary—and that density extends naturally to Ch. 4's four-consecutive-16th runs.

Two things this rule is not:

- **It is not "hit target bpm."** The 110 bpm target on Ch. 3 Core exercises stays in place as the ceiling for this chapter. Unlock bpm is a floor for opening Ch. 4, not a ceiling for Ch. 3.
- **It does not gate all Ch. 3 exercises.** Only Ex. 3.12 gates. Ex. 3.1, Ex. 3.3, Ex. 3.5 and Ex. 3.7 can be behind 82 bpm at unlock time—the transition needs Ex. 3.12 at 82, not the whole Core.

When Ex. 3.12 reaches 82 bpm (two consecutive passes at 82), open [Chapter 4](chapter-04.md) and add Ch. 4 to your active list per the tracking-document header format ([Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking)). What happens to Ch. 3 at that moment is a choice, not a rule:

- **Common path—Ch. 3 ages out.** Move Ch. 3's per-chapter working state to the archive ([Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking)); Ch. 4 replaces Ch. 3 in the focus and application blocks. Simpler cognitive load and matches the strict-sequence framing of the 16th-note density ladder in [Approach §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression).
- **Overlap path—Ch. 3 stays active alongside Ch. 4 briefly.** The active-chapters rule ([Approach §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression)) allows up to two later-stage chapters at once, so Ch. 3 and Ch. 4 can coexist if you want to keep advancing the non-gate Core exercises (Ex. 3.1, 3.3, 3.5, 3.7) towards their 110 bpm target. Split the focus block across sessions—some for Ch. 3, most for Ch. 4.

If unsure, take the common path. Ch. 3 material is not lost by aging out; three-16th runs are present in every subsequent Stage 2 chapter and stay in play through the density ladder.

## Log line format

Log this chapter's work in your tracking document ([Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking)) with the exercise number prefixed by `3.`. Beats and Fills carry no variant axis, so a bare `3.5  56 bpm  5 min  pass  even` is the full form; the Warm-Ups (Ex. 3.1–3.4) carry a variant suffix per [Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking), so Ex. 3.1 variant 1 at 90 bpm logs as `3.1.1  90 bpm  15 min  pass  even`. A short trailing comment is fine either way: `3.5  56 bpm  5 min  pass  even, weak foot solid on the run`. See [Approach §8](approach.md#8-tracking) for the full tag-plus-optional-prose convention. Log only what you actually practised; there is no obligation to touch every exercise in every session.
