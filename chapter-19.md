# R&L Ch. 19—Ternary Beats and Fills

- **Stage:** 6 (Rondinelli additions, fifth chapter)
- **Book pages:** R&L pp. 85–91
- **Total exercises:** ~77—Beats 1–67 (1-bar to 4-bar patterns), Fills 1–10 (and further)
- **Section labels present:** Beats, Fills

**Session role:** focus block.

Every session opens with warm-up on the current R&L Ch. 1 exercises before you work this chapter. Baseline 30-min session: ~7–8 min warm-up (Ch. 1) → ~15 min focus (this chapter) → ~5–6 min application → ~2 min cool-down. Full detail in [approach.md §6](approach.md#6-the-session-template).

## How to use this sheet

Fifth chapter of Stage 6 ([approach.md §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression)). This is the triplet-feel companion to Ch. 17—triplet beats and fills of different lengths (1 to 4 bars), some continuous, some broken up. Like Ch. 17, it is a library to draw from rather than a checklist to work exhaustively.

R&L's coaching for this chapter is brief and worth carrying every session: stay relaxed and focused, and if a beat is too difficult, feel free to move on and come back to it later. That explicitly permits rotating rather than grinding.

The bpm defaults come from the triplet-material row of [approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule) (start 40 bpm, target ~110 bpm). Because these are extended triplet phrases, expect the placement-focus tempos to sit lower than the endurance-focus tempos.

## Reference (discretionary)

Beats 2–4, 6–14, 16, 18–30, 32–42, 44–67 and Fills 2, 4, 6–8, 10 onward are the remaining library. R&L is explicit that you can move around freely—sample when a specific beat or fill catches your ear, or when you need a triplet-feel fill for another chapter's application block.

## Practice notes

- Stay relaxed. Triplet-feel double bass tightens the hips faster than 16th-note material—check for tension before you check bpm.
- R&L explicitly permits skipping a difficult beat and coming back to it. Take them up on that—grinding a stubborn beat is exactly the failure mode [approach.md §2](approach.md#2-a-sustainable-regimen) warns against.
- Continuous-triplet beats (long strings of triplet 8ths in the feet) are endurance tests. Broken-up beats are placement tests. Both matter; do not favour one at the expense of the other.
- Snare-and-bass unison rules from Ch. 17 still apply here—do not flam.
