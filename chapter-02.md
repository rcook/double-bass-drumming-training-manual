# R&L Ch. 2—Two Consecutive 16th Notes

- **Stage:** 2 (16th-note density ladder, first step)
- **Book pages:** R&L pp. 8–11
- **Total exercises:** 36—Warm-Ups 1–4, Beats 5–20, Fills 21–36
- **Section labels present:** Warm-Ups, Beats, Fills

**Session role:** focus and application blocks. Every session opens with warm-up on the current R&L Ch. 1 exercises before you work this chapter. Baseline 30-min session: ~7–8 min warm-up (Ch. 1) → ~15 min focus (one Ch. 2 exercise) → ~5–6 min application (one Ch. 2 beat or fill) → ~2 min cool-down. Full detail in [approach.md §6](approach.md#6-the-session-template).

## How to use this sheet

This is the first chapter of the 16th-note density ladder (Stage 2 in [approach.md §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression)). Two consecutive 16ths between the feet is the smallest unit that forces the weak foot into the same job as the strong foot. R&L notes that although two-note ideas can be played with one foot, the ability to play two evenly and powerfully between the feet is what has to be developed here—that framing is exactly what this sheet trains.

Work the Warm-Ups first. They are density drills—the point is not musicality but making the two feet sound identical on the doubled 16ths. Only once the Warm-Ups feel even should you move to the Beats, which put the doubled 16ths inside a groove. Fills are the last thing to touch and stay mostly reference—one or two is enough.

The 36 exercises are not a checklist. Each session's focus block works *one* exercise, not all five in the Core—rotate through them across sessions so all five get worked over time. The **core** list below is where most of your focus and application time goes. The **rotation** list adds variety when the core feels stale or when a specific pattern is coming up in your listening. The **reference** exercises (Beats 13–20 and most Fills) are R&L's raw material; treat them as discretionary.

The bpm defaults below come from the 16th-note beats row of [approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule): start 50 bpm, target ~110 bpm. Warm-Ups start at the same 50 bpm because the coordination cost is what makes them hard, not the speed.

**Increment differs by exercise type.** Per the advancement rule in [approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule), the Warm-Up exercises (Ex. 1 and Ex. 3) are feet-only density drills and advance at **+4 bpm** per two-pass advancement; the Beat exercises (Ex. 5, Ex. 7 and Ex. 10) are played in context with hands and advance at **+2 bpm**. The +4 ladder from 50 lands on 50, 54, 58, 62, 66, 70, 74, 78, 82; the +2 ladder from 50 lands on 50, 52, 54, 56, …, 80, 82. Both reach 82 exactly, so no ladder-skip corner case applies to this chapter—the +2 Beat ladder lands cleanly on the 82 bpm unlock (see "Moving on to Ch. 3" below).

## Your first Ch. 2 session

Your first Ch. 2 session is your first session after Stage 2 unlocks (Ex. 1, Ex. 10 and Ex. 11 of Ch. 1 all reached their 90 bpm unlock—see [chapter-01.md](chapter-01.md#moving-on-to-stage-2) "Moving on to Stage 2"). Session shape changes at the same moment: from "Ch. 1 is the whole session" to the three-block template from [approach.md §6](approach.md#6-the-session-template)—warm-up (Ch. 1), focus (Ch. 2), application (Ch. 2), cool-down.

This concrete walkthrough is what the session runbook in [approach.md §7](approach.md#7-running-a-session) looks like when applied to Ch. 2 for the first time; on later sessions the same shape applies with the bpm coming from your tracking document rather than from this sheet.

1. Open your tracking document ([approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking)). Update the header block: your active chapters now include R&L Ch. 2 alongside R&L Ch. 1. Add a per-chapter working-state subsection for Ch. 2 (see [approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking))—empty for now; you will populate rows as you touch each exercise.
2. Sit at the kit with a metronome and a timer within reach. Decide the session duration and mentally scale the block minutes per [approach.md §6](approach.md#6-the-session-template).
3. **Warm-up block (~7–8 min in a 30-min session).** Run the Ch. 1 Core as before—Ex. 1.1 at your current bpm (~2 min), Ex. 1.10 at your current bpm (~3 min), Ex. 1.11 at your current bpm (~3 min). These exercises keep advancing towards their 120 bpm target inside the warm-up block; log each one as you would have on a Stage 1 session ([approach.md §7 warm-up block](approach.md#warm-up-block)).
4. **Focus block (~15 min).** **Pick Ex. 1**—the first Ch. 2 Warm-Up, doubled 16ths on beat + e (strong-foot lead), from the Core table below. Set the metronome to 50 bpm (the starting bpm from the sheet). Set a 15-minute timer. Apply the focus-block loop from [approach.md §7](approach.md#focus-block): hold each rung for a 1-minute clean-pass attempt and, if clean, raise the metronome +4 bpm (the Warm-Up increment, [approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule)) to the next rung. The verdict criterion at each rung is evenness—are the two feet identical on the doubled 16ths?—not speed. Because Stage 2 opens only after Ex. 1, Ex. 10 and Ex. 11 of Ch. 1 pass at 90 bpm ([chapter-01.md](chapter-01.md#moving-on-to-stage-2)), 50 bpm on a feet-only doubled-16th drill sits well below your existing coordination floor and you should expect to climb several rungs on session 1. If you feel pulled to skip past the +4 increment—jumping straight to a much higher rung to save time—that is a signal you may not have earned the Ch. 1 unlock cleanly; drop back to Ch. 1 and re-verify the unlock rather than papering over it here. Add one log entry recording the highest bpm at which you passed cleanly: `2.1  <bpm> bpm  15 min  <verdict>  <note>`. Then update the working state for Ex. 1 per [approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking). On this first-ever session, that means `last pass bpm` ← this session's ceiling and `current bpm` ← this session's ceiling directly (waiting for confirmation next time). On subsequent Ex. 1 sessions, §8's subsequent-session rule kicks in: `current bpm` ← `min(this session's ceiling, previous session's ceiling) + 4 bpm` (the Warm-Up increment).
5. **Application block (~5–6 min).** **Pick Ex. 5**—the first Ch. 2 Beat, doubled 16ths inside a groove—from the Core table. Set the metronome to 50 bpm. Set a 5-minute timer. Same loop, at the +2 bpm Beat increment: hold each rung for a minute and raise +2 if clean. Ex. 5 puts hands into the pattern for the first time in this chapter, so unlike Ex. 1 your ceiling may emerge early—that is fine; log where the exercise stops being clean. Add one log entry recording the highest bpm passed cleanly: `2.5  <bpm> bpm  5 min  <verdict>  <note>`. Then update the working state for Ex. 5 per [approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking). On this first-ever session, that means `last pass bpm` ← this session's ceiling and `current bpm` ← this session's ceiling directly (waiting for confirmation next time). On subsequent Ex. 5 sessions, §8's subsequent-session rule kicks in: `current bpm` ← `min(this session's ceiling, previous session's ceiling) + 2 bpm` (the Beat increment).
6. **Cool-down (~2 min).** No metronome, no timer. Play something musical. Nothing to log.
7. Stop.

That is a complete first Ch. 2 session. You have not touched Ex. 3, Ex. 7 or Ex. 10—those come in later sessions as you rotate through the Core.

**On your second session**, look up the bpm and verdict for each exercise from session one. The advancement rule ([approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule)) is *two consecutive `pass` sessions at the same bpm* before you raise the metronome—and it applies per exercise, so Ex. 1 and Ex. 5 advance on their own two-pass counts. Options for session two:

- **Repeat the same pair (Ex. 1 focus, Ex. 5 application).** Set the metronome to each exercise's current working bpm from session one (looked up in your tracking document per [approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking)) and run the same [approach.md §7 focus-block loop](approach.md#focus-block) again—hold, probe, hold, probe. If both `pass` at their session-one bpm, a second `pass` earns the raise (+4 for Ex. 1, +2 for Ex. 5) on session three.
- **Rotate to a new pair.** Focus Ex. 3 (the other Warm-Up) at 50 bpm; application Ex. 7 or Ex. 10 (a fresh Beat) at 50 bpm. Run the same §7 hold-and-probe loop from minute one on each and log the highest bpm you passed cleanly. This gives you a first-time reading on two more Core exercises but leaves Ex. 1 and Ex. 5 waiting for their second pass.

Both are valid. Consistency beats coverage in the first fortnight—if you are unsure, repeat the same pair until each Core exercise has two passes on the board, then rotate.

## Rotating through the Core

The Ch. 2 Core has five exercises but each session's focus block holds one (two on longer days) and each session's application block holds one. That means five exercises need to share your focus and application time across a training week. A workable pattern for the first two or three weeks, at roughly five sessions a week:

- **Focus** alternates between Ex. 1 and Ex. 3 (the two Warm-Ups). One per session, alternating.
- **Application** cycles through Ex. 5, Ex. 7 and Ex. 10 (the three Beats). One per session.

That is one session per Warm-Up every second day and one session per Beat every third day—enough to accumulate two-pass advancements on each without any single exercise sitting stale for a week. Adjust to fit your schedule; the mechanical loop from [approach.md §7](approach.md#7-running-a-session) applies unchanged regardless of which Core exercise is in the block.

**Worked advancement example, Ex. 1 (Warm-Up, +4 bpm ladder).** Session A `pass` at 50 bpm; session B (next Ex. 1 session) `pass` at 50 → raise; session C raise to 54, `pass`; session D `pass` at 54 → raise; session E raise to 58, `pass`… The two passes accrue across sessions on the days you actually work Ex. 1, not back-to-back days on the calendar—if Ex. 1 lands every second day, the two passes are two days apart. Per [approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule) that is if anything a stronger confirmation than a same-day repeat.

**Worked advancement example, Ex. 10 (Beat, +2 bpm ladder).** Same shape, but each raise is +2 bpm instead of +4. Session A `pass` at 50; session B (next Ex. 10 session) `pass` at 50 → raise; session C raise to 52, `pass`; session D `pass` at 52 → raise; session E raise to 54… The +2 ladder reaches 82 (the Ch. 3 unlock bpm) in 16 increments; assume roughly one advancement per week per exercise once things are stable, so plan for Ex. 10 to reach 82 over three-to-four months of steady work.

## Core (most sessions)

Your focus and application pool for this chapter. Pick one exercise for the focus block and one Beat for the application block each session, rotating across sessions so all five get worked over time. Concretely: for the chosen exercise, look up your current bpm in the tracking document (or use the starting bpm for the first time), set the metronome, set a timer for the block minutes, play unbroken until the timer stops, log a verdict per the clean-pass rule ([approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule)), then move on to the next block.

| Ex. | Section | Role | Start | Unlock | Target | Increment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Warm-Ups | Doubled 16ths on beat + e, strong-foot lead | 50 bpm |  | 110 bpm | +4 bpm |
| 3 | Warm-Ups | Doubled 16ths on & + a, strong-foot lead | 50 bpm |  | 110 bpm | +4 bpm |
| 5 | Beats | Rock beat, doubled 16ths on 3 + 3e, strong-foot lead | 50 bpm |  | 110 bpm | +2 bpm |
| 7 | Beats | Rock beat, doubled 16ths on 3& + 3a plus single strong-foot kicks on 1, 1& and 3, strong-foot lead | 50 bpm |  | 110 bpm | +2 bpm |
| 10 | Beats | Rock beat, doubled 16ths on 2& + 2a and 4& + 4a plus single strong-foot kicks on 1& and 3&, strong-foot lead | 50 bpm | 82 bpm | 110 bpm | +2 bpm |

**Why these five?** R&L Ch. 2 has 36 exercises but most are variations on the doubled-16th idea. All five Core exercises use a strong-foot lead on the doubled 16ths; they differ on **where in the beat the two 16ths land** (front vs back) and, for the Beats, **how many times per bar** the doubled 16ths appear. Ex. 1 and Ex. 3 are the two Warm-Ups that isolate the doubled-16th foot pattern before hands enter—Ex. 1 places the two 16ths on beat + e (front of beat), Ex. 3 on & + a (back of beat), each repeated on every beat of the bar. Ex. 5, Ex. 7 and Ex. 10 are three Beats that carry the same front-vs-back placement axis inside a standard rock groove (hi-hat 8ths, snare on 2 and 4): Ex. 5 puts the doubled 16ths on beat 3 + 3e (front of beat 3, 3 kicks per bar), Ex. 7 on 3& + 3a with additional single kicks on 1, 1& and 3 (back of beat 3, 5 kicks per bar) and Ex. 10 on 2& + 2a and 4& + 4a with single kicks on 1& and 3& (back of beats 2 and 4, 6 kicks per bar). The three Beats form a front-to-back placement axis on the doubled 16ths and a density ladder on total kicks per bar, with Ex. 10 sitting at both extremes—highest density, latest doubled-16th placement—which is why it gates the transition to Ch. 3 (see "Moving on to Ch. 3" below).

## Rotation (some sessions)

Once the Core is stable (say, two weeks of consistent `pass` verdicts across Ex. 1, Ex. 3, Ex. 5, Ex. 7 and Ex. 10), you can start dropping in a Rotation exercise. Two ways to use it: swap it in *for* a Core exercise on a given session (so the total block count stays the same), or add it as a fourth block if you have session time to spare. Do not stack more than one Rotation exercise on the same day; the Core is the main path.

| Ex. | Section | Role | Start | Target | Increment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | Warm-Ups | Variant footing on doubled 16ths | 50 bpm | 110 bpm | +4 bpm |
| 4 | Warm-Ups | Variant footing on doubled 16ths | 50 bpm | 110 bpm | +4 bpm |
| 12 | Beats | Groove-context reinforcement | 50 bpm | 110 bpm | +2 bpm |
| 21 | Fills | One-measure fill with doubled 16ths | 50 bpm | 110 bpm | +2 bpm |
| 24 | Fills | One-measure fill with doubled 16ths | 50 bpm | 110 bpm | +2 bpm |

**Cadence.** Aim for each Rotation exercise to get worked once every couple of weeks or so when time allows—about one Rotation slot per practice week at the ~5-days-per-week baseline. That is a target, not a rule: skip Rotation entirely on short days, and a missed fortnight is not a debt. The advancement rule still applies—two consecutive `pass` sessions at the same bpm earn the raise—but the two sessions accrue across weeks rather than back-to-back days.

## Reference (discretionary)

Beats 13–20 and Fills 22–36 are reference material. R&L flags Beats 13–18 as harder and says you can come back to them later; Beats 16–18 use syncopated snare rhythms; Beats 19–20 are two-bar phrases meant as combining fodder. Fills 21–30 are one-measure ideas, Fill 31 is a two-bar phrase with a short fill, Fills 32–33 are phrases containing a one-bar fill and Fill 34 is longer than a measure. Dip into these when you want variety or when your ear picks one out—do not treat them as a checklist. If you never play them, that is fine.

## Practice notes

- The Warm-Ups exist to make the two feet sound the same on the doubled 16ths. If the weak foot is dropping, do not raise the bpm—the ceiling is set by the weaker side.
- Try the Fills with different orchestrations. R&L suggests strong hand on snare and weak hand on high tom, or strong hand on floor tom and weak hand on high tom.
- Beats 19–20 and Fills 31–34 are examples of phrasing, not required drills. Use them once the shorter material is clean.
- Do not chase Beats 13–18 early. They are marked harder for a reason; hold them until Beats 5–12 hold ~90 bpm.
- These exercises can be played on the kit or on a practice pad—tapping the pattern on the floor is legitimate practice. See [approach.md §7 "Off-kit session"](approach.md#off-kit-session) for how off-kit reps sit against the clean-pass rule and how to log them if you want to.
- If the target bpm feels far away, that is expected. The number is a ceiling, not a schedule.

## Moving on to Ch. 3

Ch. 3 (three consecutive 16ths between the feet—see [approach.md §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression)) unlocks when **Ex. 10 reaches its unlock bpm of 82 bpm**—two consecutive `pass` sessions at 82 bpm on Ex. 10, per the advancement rule in [approach.md §4](approach.md#4-the-tempo-target-and-clean-pass-rule). Ex. 10 is a Beat and advances at +2 bpm; the +2 ladder from 50 lands on 50, 52, 54, …, 80, 82—so 82 is on the ladder and no rounding is needed.

Only one gate exercise, not three. Within-stage transitions inside the 16th-note density ladder (Ch. 2 → 3 → 4 → 5) are quantitative—denser runs of the same subdivision—rather than qualitative, so one checkpoint is enough per [approach.md §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression) (unlock scheme, rule 2). Ex. 10 is the gate because it has the highest doubled-16th density per bar of any Ch. 2 Core exercise—doubled 16ths on both 2& + 2a and 4& + 4a, i.e. twice per bar versus once per bar in Ex. 5 and Ex. 7.

Two things this rule is not:

- **It is not "hit target bpm."** The 110 bpm target on Ch. 2 Core exercises stays in place as the ceiling for this chapter. Unlock bpm is a floor for opening Ch. 3, not a ceiling for Ch. 2.
- **It does not gate all Ch. 2 exercises.** Only Ex. 10 gates. Ex. 1, Ex. 3, Ex. 5 and Ex. 7 can be behind 82 bpm at unlock time—the transition needs Ex. 10 at 82, not the whole Core.

When Ex. 10 reaches 82 bpm (two consecutive passes at 82), open [chapter-03.md](chapter-03.md) and add Ch. 3 to your active list per the tracking-document header format ([approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking)). What happens to Ch. 2 at that moment is a choice, not a rule:

- **Common path—Ch. 2 ages out.** Move Ch. 2's per-chapter working state to the archive ([approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking)); Ch. 3 replaces Ch. 2 in the focus and application blocks. Simpler cognitive load and matches the strict-sequence framing of the 16th-note density ladder in [approach.md §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression).
- **Overlap path—Ch. 2 stays active alongside Ch. 3 briefly.** The active-chapters rule ([approach.md §5](approach.md#5-the-chapter-progression)) allows up to two later-stage chapters at once, so Ch. 2 and Ch. 3 can coexist if you want to keep advancing the non-gate Core exercises (Ex. 1, Ex. 3, Ex. 5, Ex. 7) towards their 110 bpm target. Split the focus block across sessions—some for Ch. 2, most for Ch. 3.

If unsure, take the common path. Ch. 2 material is not lost by aging out; the doubled-16th pattern is present in every subsequent Stage 2 chapter and stays in play through the density ladder.

Log this chapter's work in your tracking document ([approach.md §8](approach.md#8-tracking)) with the exercise number prefixed by `2.`—for example `2.5  56 bpm  5 min  pass  even`, or with a short trailing comment: `2.5  56 bpm  5 min  pass  even, weak foot solid on the sustained bar`.
