# Edition notes

> **Status: evolving.** This list is populated as I work through the source book. It will not be complete until my reading of Rondinelli & Lauren's *Encyclopedia of Double Bass Drumming* is complete. Entries below reflect what I have personally verified against both the Original and Revised editions; anything not yet checked is not on the list.

## What this file covers

Four kinds of entry:

- **Printing errors** — cases where one edition prints an exercise in a way that appears to contradict the chapter's stated purpose or R&L's own prose, and the other edition prints it in a way that fits.
- **Per-exercise notation differences** — cases where the two editions notate the same exercise in genuinely different but not obviously wrong ways.
- **Confirmed identical, worth mentioning** — cases where an exercise looks unusual enough to raise a "did they typo this?" question, and I have checked both editions and can confirm the notation stands as printed in both.
- **Structural inventory** — one-line acknowledgements of macro-level differences between the two editions (e.g. Revised-only chapters).

The general edition policy (which edition the manual defaults to, how to work from the Original Edition, etc.) is in [Approach §3.2](approach.md#32-original-edition-compatibility), not here.

## Printing errors

### Ex. 1.1, bar 2 (Revised Edition)

- **Symptom.** The Revised Edition prints bar 2 of Ex. 1.1 on the same bass drum as bar 1 — both on BD I (strong foot).
- **Why this looks wrong.** R&L Ch. 1's stated purpose is to "isolate and help develop your weak foot." A two-bar exercise with both bars on the strong foot does not serve that purpose.
- **What the Original Edition prints.** Bar 2 on BD II (weak foot). Reads as a strong-foot / weak-foot alternation across the two bars, which does fit the chapter's stated purpose.
- **How the manual plays it.** Per the Original Edition: bar 1 strong foot, bar 2 weak foot. Called out on [Chapter 1](chapter-01.md) under "Note on Ex. 1.1 (edition discrepancy)".

None confirmed to date beyond the Ex. 1.1 printing error above (which is an error, not a stylistic difference). Add here as more surface during ongoing reading.

## Confirmed identical, worth mentioning

### Ex. 5.13, snare on 2e and 4

- **What raised the flag.** The snare voice in Ex. 5.13 reads `_____x______x___` — hits on 2e and 4, not the 2-and-4 backbeat that Ex. 2.5, Ex. 2.7, Ex. 2.10, Ex. 3.12 and Ex. 4.12 all share.
- **Verification.** Checked bar-by-bar against both the Original and Revised editions. Identical in both. The manual accepts the notation as an intentional Ch. 5 stylistic choice — probably driven by the placement of the five-consecutive-16th run at the top of the bar (positions 0–4, spanning beat 1 into beat 2), which the 2-and-4 backbeat would occlude.
- **Implication for practice.** Play as printed. Do not "correct" the snare to a 2-and-4 backbeat.
