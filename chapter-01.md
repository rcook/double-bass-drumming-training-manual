# R&L Ch. 1 — Starters

- **Stage:** 1 (foundation, always active as warm-up)
- **Book pages:** R&L pp. 6–7
- **Total exercises:** 13, all under a single "Beats" section
- **Section labels present:** Beats

**Session role:** this chapter has two roles depending on where you are in the programme.

- **Before you unlock Stage 2** (i.e. you are still working only R&L Ch. 1): this chapter *is* the whole session. Warm-up, focus and application collapse into working the exercises below.
- **Once Stage 2 is active** (any later chapter is in your active list): this chapter becomes the warm-up block only, at the top of every session. Full session shape and time allocation are in `approach.md` §6.

## How to use this sheet

This chapter is the permanent warm-up. It is never fully graduated out of — the exercises just get faster and stay in rotation for the life of the manual. See `approach.md` §5, Stage 1.

The 13 exercises are not a checklist. You do not work through all of them every session; you rotate. The **core** list below is where most of your time goes. The **rotation** list adds variety and density on days when you want a fuller warm-up. The **reference** exercises (2–9) are accent-variation studies — R&L includes them as raw material; treat them as discretionary. Play them when they look interesting, skip them when they do not.

The book itself notes (R&L p. 6) that exercises 8–12 are single-stroke rolls between the feet, and calls single-stroke rolls "the foundation of double bass drumming". The curated subset below reflects that guidance.

## Core (most sessions)

These three are the daily minimum for this chapter. If you have 5 minutes for warm-up, do these.

| Ex. | Pattern | Start | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Quarter-note alternating between feet | 60 bpm | 120 bpm |
| 10 | 16th-note single-stroke roll between feet | 60 bpm | 120 bpm |
| 11 | 8th-note-triplet single-stroke roll between feet | 60 bpm | 120 bpm |

## Rotation (some sessions)

Add one or both of these when you want a longer or more demanding warm-up. Rotate rather than adding both every day.

| Ex. | Pattern | Start | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12 | Sextuplet single-stroke roll between feet | 50 bpm | 100 bpm |
| 13 | 32nd-note single-stroke roll between feet | 40 bpm | 60 bpm |

## Reference (discretionary)

Exercises 2–9 are quarter-note and 8th-note patterns exploring accent placement between the feet. Useful for accent control; not required for the strength-and-accuracy path. Dip in when you want variety or when a specific accent pattern shows up in a beat you are working elsewhere. If you never play them, that is fine.

## Practice notes

- The book's guidance to hold each exercise for at least 1 minute (R&L p. 6) is the same standard as the clean-pass rule (`approach.md` §4). Do not shortcut it.
- These exercises can be played on the kit or on a practice pad — tapping the pattern on the floor is legitimate practice.
- Do not neglect the accents where they are written. Even at slow tempos, an unaccented pass does not count as clean.
- If the target bpm feels far away, that is expected. The number is a ceiling, not a schedule.

## Log line format

Log this chapter's work in your tracking document (`approach.md` §7) with the exercise number prefixed by `1.` — for example `1.10  84 bpm  pass  even`. Log only what you actually practised; there is no obligation to touch every exercise in every session.
